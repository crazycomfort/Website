AC Drain Line Clogged? Here's What to Know

Published January 22, 2026 • 4 min read

Your AC doesn't just cool air—it also removes humidity. That moisture has to go somewhere, and it drains through a condensate line. When that line gets clogged, you can end up with water damage, system shutdowns, and mold growth.

Signs Your AC Drain Is Clogged

Water Around Your Indoor Unit

If you see water pooling around your indoor air handler or furnace, the drain line is likely backed up. The condensate pan is overflowing because water can't escape through the clogged line.

AC Shuts Off Unexpectedly

Many modern systems have a float switch that shuts down the AC when the drain pan fills up. This is a safety feature to prevent water damage. If your AC keeps shutting off, a clogged drain could be the culprit.

Musty or Moldy Smell

Standing water in a clogged drain line creates a perfect environment for mold and mildew growth. If you notice a musty smell when your AC runs, the drain line should be inspected.

Water Stains on Ceiling or Walls

If your air handler is in the attic (common in Texas homes), a clogged drain can cause water damage to ceilings and walls below before you realize there's a problem.

What Causes Drain Line Clogs?

  • Algae and mold growth: The most common cause. The dark, moist environment inside the drain line is perfect for growth.
  • Dust and debris: Dirt that bypasses your air filter can accumulate in the drain pan and line.
  • Insect nests: Bugs sometimes build nests in the drain line exit point.

Can I Clear It Myself?

Some homeowners try DIY methods like pouring vinegar down the drain line or using a wet/dry vacuum. These can work for minor clogs, but:

  • You may not clear the clog completely
  • You could damage the line or connections
  • You might miss an underlying issue causing frequent clogs

If DIY methods don't work, or if the clog keeps coming back, it's time to call a professional.
